# Plan: an HD-2D look for DS games

Started 11-21. Phases 1 (bloom and vignette) and 2 (real depth of field, OpenGL only) are done and checked on the Fold (below). Phase 3 (shading and light) has five steps, A to E, all built, checked on the desktop and the Fold, and merged; the guard band's 8x fix is checked on the Fold and waiting to merge ("Where things stand" under step D). Open decisions are marked **Decide**.

## The goal

HD-2D is the look from Octopath Traveler and the Dragon Quest remakes: crisp pixel-art characters in a 3D world, with depth of field that blurs the near and far parts of the scene, bloom on bright spots, soft light, a vignette and strong color grading. A lot of DS games are already halfway there. The Pokémon field maps are 3D models with flat characters standing in them, and the camera looks down at an angle, so the same post effects should carry over well.

The aim is an opt-in look per game, driven by a Lua mod the way tilt-shift is today, that runs on the phone and the desktop app alike. The game never sees any of it.

Decided 11-21: the first target is the Pokémon White field, since we already know how it draws its maps (below). The reference scene is the city plaza from the user's save (map chunk 10). White 2 and Platinum already have tilt-shift scripts to compare against later.

## What we have already

- **Tilt-shift** (`melon.screen.tilt_shift`, [MODS.md](../MODS.md)): a sharp band across the top screen with a blur that grows towards the edges, plus a saturation and contrast boost. It's two passes after the filter in `VideoFilterPipeline.kt`, on the finished frame, so it blurs 2D text too. This is a fake depth of field: the band is a guess at where the player is, not real depth. Examples are in `docs/examples/lua/tilt_shift_*.lua`.
- **The same passes on the desktop.** Phase 3 step A of [plan-desktop-app.md](plan-desktop-app.md) ported `VideoFilterPipeline` to C++, and `DesktopShaderExportTest` keeps the shaders byte-identical, so new passes written once land in both.
- **A depth buffer.** The OpenGL 3D renderer draws into `DepthBufferTex` (`GL_DEPTH24_STENCIL8`, at the 3D scale) for its edge and fog passes. It's DS depth (Z or W buffer per polygon), not linear distance, and it never leaves the renderer today. Compute keeps depth too: its result buffer (`ResultValue` from `ResultDepthStart` in `GPU3D_Compute_shaders.h`) holds two depth layers per pixel, and the final pass already reads them for edge marking and fog, so it could write a depth image next to `FinalFB` with one more `imageStore`.
- **Hi-res 3D.** The 3D is already drawn at up to 8x, so the world gets sharp while the 2D stays at native resolution, which is half of the HD-2D contrast for free.
- **Pokémon White map knowledge** (from the 11-21 RE session, not written up in the repo yet): the field draws its map chunks and buildings through one display-list send at `0x206dc18`, every send matches a ROM display list by hash, and the overworld runs at 30 fps. That's the hook for swapping in better models later, which is a separate project (see the last phase).

## Phases

### Phase 1: screen-space effects, no depth

Add bloom and a vignette as passes in `VideoFilterPipeline`, next to tilt-shift, with a Lua call like `melon.screen.bloom(strength[, opts])`. Bloom is a bright-pass, a downscaled blur and an add; the vignette is one multiply. Both work on the finished frame like tilt-shift, so 2D gets them too, and they follow the same rules: built on first use, skipped while off, newest script wins, gone when the script stops.

Built 11-21: `melon.screen.bloom()` and `melon.screen.vignette()` ([MODS.md](../MODS.md)), the desktop's `--bloom` flag, and `docs/examples/lua/hd2d_white.lua`. On the desktop it's checked with `--bloom` only, since the desktop doesn't run Lua scripts. Without `--bloom` the desktop's screenshots are byte-identical to before, and with it only the top screen changes.

First look on White's field (OpenGL 4x, the city street from the user's save): the glow, vignette and tilt-shift stack as intended. But a daytime White street has no lamps, and the pale pavement sits around luma 0.8, so a brightness threshold mostly lifts the floor: at 0.6 the street washes out, at 0.8 it's milder but still the floor that glows. The example's threshold default went from 0.7 to 0.8 for that. Picking real light sources (windows, lamps, night scenes) needs more than brightness, which ties into phase 3.

**On the Fold, 11-21** (USB, plugged in; White from the user's save, standing still in Striaton City, WideMelon on at 342 wide; `perf_logs/2026-11-21-hd2d-bloom/`): `hd2d_white.lua` loads with no errors, the top screen gets the glow, vignette and tilt-shift, the bottom screen is pixel-identical on and off, the menu clears it and it fades back. No FPS drop and no missed frames anywhere; the script costs about 0.2 ms of work per frame and a little GPU: +0.6 Mcycles a frame on OpenGL 4x (2.6 → 3.2), +2.3 at 8x (5.7 → 8.0), +0.7 on Compute 4x (5.5 → 6.2), rough numbers since the governor moves clocks. No build or target errors in any run, and the xbr2 and FXAA filters draw it the same way. From the user's own states the same day: a town and a route show the look, a battle shows none of it, the hold L, tap R toggle works by hand, and a text box keeps only the vignette. Phase 1 is done.

This is cheap to build and shows quickly whether the look is worth the deeper work. Done when a White script with tilt-shift, bloom and vignette runs on the Fold and the desktop with the same picture, and the phone's frame time hasn't moved much (to measure: bloom's blur at 4x and 8x on the Fold).

Decided 11-21 (phase 3 step B): bloom only touches the 3D where the frame has its depth, so white text boxes don't glow. `only_3d = false` gives the old glow on everything.

### Phase 2: real depth of field

Swap tilt-shift's fixed band for real depth: the blur follows how far each pixel is from a focus distance. That needs three things:

1. **Depth out of the 3D renderer.** Hand the depth to `GLCompositor` next to the 3D color: OpenGL's `DepthBufferTex` as it is, Compute's from the final pass (above). Both renderers already keep a kept and a spare 3D buffer for the early capture readback, so the depth needs the same pair or it'll belong to the wrong frame.
2. **Depth through to the app.** The app only gets one texture per frame: the compositor's output, both screens stacked, handed over in the frame queue (`PresentFrameWrapper.textureId`). Depth needs a second texture that travels with it, one per frame-queue buffer, laid out the same way (the WideMelon wings and margin rows included). Two things to get right there: with the output cap on (K4) the output is smaller than the 3D, and the compositor's `DOWN3D` programs average the 3D pixels under each output pixel, which is wrong for depth, so depth should take the nearest one instead. And pixels where 2D is on top need a rule: they stay sharp (text, menus) or take the depth of the 3D under them. The compositor knows which layer won each pixel, so it can write that in the same texture.
3. **A focus point.** The Lua script gives the focus, either as a depth or as a screen point to sample, such as the player's position on screen. Whether a mod can find that position for White isn't known yet.

**Checked 11-21: White's field characters are 3D.** On the desktop (OpenGL, booted from the user's save into a city street), the player's pixel edges at 4x land on all four sub-pixel positions, the same as the buildings and trees, where a 2D sprite's would all sit on the 4-pixel grid. So the characters are polygons in the 3D scene, they have depth, and the blur will treat them like the rest of the world. Their texels stay blocky at any scale, which is the pixel-art half of the look already. The bottom screen in the field is all 2D.

Done when the White plaza from the user's save shows a sharp player and nearby buildings with the far edge of the map blurred, and the blur follows the camera as it moves, on OpenGL at least.

**Built 11-21, OpenGL only** (core branch `feature/hd2d-depth`):

- *Depth out of the renderer.* While a frontend gives a depth texture, `GLRenderer` resolves each render's depth into a texture (RGBA8 since phase 3 step A) right after the frame (`ResolveDepth`), before `GLClearFast` drops it. That texture swaps with the color buffer, kept and spare included, so a late Blit reads the depth of the frame it shows. A frame skipped as identical is drawn again when there's no depth for it yet. With no depth texture given, none of this runs and the renderer frees what it had.
- *Through the compositor.* After the frame's draws, `GLCompositor::RenderDepth` draws the top half's depth into the frame's own depth texture, with the main draw's quad and the same scroll, wing and margin maths (`kCompositorDepthFS`). It takes the nearest 3D pixel, not an average, so a capped output is fine too. Its values (24-bit since step A) are 0 where 2D is on top (compmode 1, 5-7, other display modes, hi-res captured lines), which stays sharp, the largest value where the 3D is transparent, which blurs as the farthest, and the depth otherwise. `OutputLayout::HasDepth` says whether this draw wrote it.
- *To the app.* `FrameQueue` makes a per-frame depth texture (the frame's width by its top half's rows) only while `MelonInstance` wants depth, that is, a script asks for it (`LuaEngine::wantsDepth`) or the desktop's `--dof` does. It reaches Kotlin as `PresentFrameWrapper.depthTextureId` (0 for a frame without depth) and the desktop as `FrameView::DepthTexture`.
- *The blur.* `melon.screen.depth_of_field(strength[, opts])` ([MODS.md](../MODS.md)). It runs two separable passes after the filter's own and before the bloom. Each texel's radius grows with its depth's distance from the focus, and a tap only counts as far as its own blur reaches, so sharp things don't smear over blurred ones. The focus is picked once a frame by a 1 x 1 pass (`DepthOfFieldFocusShader`) that also reads the last frame's, and the blur passes read it from there. It's a fixed depth (a script's, or since 11-22 the focus textures' polygon, below), or else, in order: the nearest flat 3D at and up to 18 DS pixels above a screen point (the center by default), where flat means the same depth 3 pixels lower, which only a quad facing the camera has (White's characters; its floor changes by hundreds of 24-bit steps over 3 pixels); with none, the last focus while the nearest 3D at the point is nearer than it by more than the sharp range, for up to 255 aging steps every other frame (about 8.5 s), so a roof or pillar covering the player doesn't take the focus (the roof over her in Striaton was 0.99325 against her 0.99617); otherwise the nearest 3D at the point and the four points 3 DS pixels around it, or 6 if none of those is 3D. A frame with no 3D there has no focus and ends a hold (a door warp goes through one). Nearest in that last case, because the point's own depth alone pulsed: walking sideways in White, the center (the player's foot line) showed the floor between her legs every other step, 0.00028 behind the sprite and so past the sharp range, and the whole picture's blur flipped every 8 frames. The sprite is one flat quad at 0.996460 in every pose, and the point 3 px above the center is always on it.

### Phase 3: light and shading

Planned 11-21. Steps A, B and C are built and checked on the desktop and the Fold; D (sun shadows, replanned after C) is built and checked on the desktop, a Pixel 8 and the Fold (11-22); E is built and checked on the desktop and the Fold (11-22). The harder half of the look: soft shadows where things meet the ground, haze in the distance, and glows on lamps and windows instead of on anything bright. Some of it can come from depth alone, some only from knowing the scene. The steps go from cheap and depth-only to scene knowledge, and each one is worth having on its own.

**Why phase 2's depth wasn't enough.** It was the top 16 bits of the DS's 24-bit depth. White's city street fits in about 0.005 of the range, so the whole screen had only 314 depth values. That's enough to pick a blur radius, but shading from it would come out in bands. The full 24 bits give 68,124 values in the same frame (step A). The app also doesn't know the camera, only raw depth, so anything that needs real distances or a light direction, such as sun shadows, needs the projection too (step D).

#### Step A: the full depth and a flags byte

Change the depth texture from RG8 to RGBA8: RGB is the 24-bit depth, A is flags. Bit 0: 2D on top (stays sharp, as 0 is now). Bit 1: transparent 3D (as 65535 is now). Bit 2: the frame is W-buffered, which the core knows from `FlushAttributes` (phase 2 couldn't tell Z from W). The other bits are kept for steps D (bits 3 and 4) and E. The DOF shader decodes to the same 0..1 values, so White's DOF defaults keep working. The core changes `kDepthResolveFS`, `kCompositorDepthFS` and the texture format in `ResolveDepth` and `FrameQueue`; the app and desktop share one decode function in `ShaderProgramSource`.

Done when the DOF looks the same as before on the desktop and the Fold, and a test shows the 24 bits arriving (a smooth floor gives smooth values, not steps).

**Built 11-21** (core branch `feature/hd2d-depth24`, from `feature/hd2d-depth`). As planned, with the flags as 1, 2 and 4 in A. The W-buffer flag comes from the frame's first polygon, as the renderer's own stats take it, and rides in the resolved texture's A, so the kept and spare buffers carry it with their frame. The 0 and largest-value sentinels stay, so a shader that only reads depth needs no flags. The resolve gets back the DS's own 24-bit z exactly (`int(depth * 2^24)`: the renderer writes z / 2^24, which the depth buffer stores one lower from 2^23 up), and the app's `decodeDepth` keeps every real depth below 1.0 however the GPU divides, so an opaque far plane at the largest depth still reads as behind the 3D, as before. The desktop has `--depth-shot FILE.png` (desktop/README.md): the last frame's depth as an image, with how many distinct values it holds.

#### Step B: haze and 3D-only bloom

Two small changes that ride on passes we already have.

- **Haze.** HD-2D fades the far part of the scene towards a pale color. `melon.screen.haze(strength, { color, start, falloff })` mixes the 3D towards `color` as its depth goes past `start`, in the same raw depth units as the DOF. It's folded into bloom's combine pass, which already reads every texel once, so it costs almost nothing.
- **Bloom on the 3D only.** The flags byte says which pixels are 2D, so bloom's bright pass can skip them. White text boxes stop glowing, which settles phase 1's **Decide**: a `only_3d` bloom option, on by default when depth is there, and the old behavior on Compute and Software.

**Built 11-21.** `melon.screen.haze()` and bloom's `only_3d` ([MODS.md](../MODS.md)); the desktop's `--haze` and `--bloom-all` ([desktop/README.md](../desktop/README.md)). Both only touch 3D pixels, a depth strictly between the 2D-on-top 0 and the nothing-3D 1, so a text box isn't hazed and what's behind the 3D is left alone. The haze is one more define in bloom's combine pass, which now has up to four variants (glow or not, haze or not); the 3D-only bright pass is a define too. A script with only haze, or with a glow and `only_3d`, makes the core keep each frame's depth, as the depth of field does. Without depth (Compute, Software, a script's first frame) the haze draws nothing and the glow takes every pixel. Haze, bloom and vignette share one request, so the newest running script wins for all three. The haze defaults (start 0.9968, falloff 0.0008) suit White's field, and `hd2d_white.lua` has a haze option at 0.6, kept for text like the depth of field.

#### Step C: depth shading (soft contact shadows)

Darken the places where something sits in front of what's behind it: the base of walls, under roofs, around the player's feet, between trees. This is "unsharp masking the depth buffer" (Luft, Colditz and Deussen, SIGGRAPH 2006): blur the depth, and where a pixel is farther away than its blurred neighborhood, it's in a corner or behind something, so it gets darker. It only needs raw depth, no camera, and the blur is separable like the DOF's, so it's two passes plus a multiply.

To keep it cheap at 8x, the depth blur runs at DS resolution (256 x 192 plus the wings), as bloom's blur does, and the darkening is scaled back up with a depth check so edges don't get a halo. API: `melon.screen.shade(strength, { radius, range, color })`, tuned per game in raw depth like the DOF. It isn't true ambient occlusion, since it doesn't know distances in world units, but with a fixed camera like White's field it should look close.

Done when Striaton City shows soft shading at the base of buildings and under the player, with no halos at 8x and no flicker while walking, and the Fold still holds 60 FPS at 8x with everything on.

**Built 11-21.** `melon.screen.shade(strength, { radius, range, color })` ([MODS.md](../MODS.md)) and the desktop's `--shade` ([desktop/README.md](../desktop/README.md)). It runs after the filter's own passes and before the depth of field, so the DOF blurs the shading with the rest. Four passes, all RGBA8 so no float targets are needed: the depth goes down to DS resolution (up to 4 samples per DS pixel, only real 3D counted), gets blurred both ways there as a normalized convolution (A carries how much of the kernel was 3D), and the apply pass at full resolution compares each pixel's own depth with a hand-made bilinear read of the blurred one. Differencing against the full-res depth is the depth check, so edges stay crisp at 8x with no low-res darkening texture to scale up. Sums are kept as 24-bit differences from a reference so they stay exact in highp. The darkening is `strength * clamp((depth - blurred) / range, 0, 1)` towards the frame times `color`, and it fades out where the blur's coverage drops below a half (next to 2D, at the screen's edges), so a text box doesn't cast a shadow onto the 3D under it. Defaults: radius 8 DS pixels, range 0.0008, color 0x33384F; `hd2d_white.lua` has it at 0.7, kept during text. A range of 0.0003 or less outlines every silhouette, and 0.001 and up barely shows.

#### Step D: sun shadows

Decided 11-21, after C: C isn't the shadow the look wants. The reference is the gen1recomp voxel mod's overworld (the user's screenshot of Pokémon Yellow's Viridian City): a sun from one direction, and every building, sign and character casts a long, fairly firm shadow across the ground away from it. C only darkens corners and right behind things, since without the camera it can't throw a shadow in any direction. C stays as a soft extra under D, lower or off by default in `hd2d_white.lua` once D lands. The voxel mod's own code isn't in `D:\Dev\gen1recomp` (`docs/modding.md` names `mods/voxel_world`, which isn't in the checkout), so this goes on the screenshot alone.

**How.** Screen-space shadows: for each 3D pixel, rebuild its position in view space from the depth and the projection, step from it towards the sun, project each step back onto the screen, and if the depth there is nearer than the step (a roof, wall or sprite is in the way) the pixel is in shadow. A shadow map wouldn't do better here. The DS clips every polygon to the screen before the renderer sees it, so the polygons a shadow map would draw are the same ones the depth already holds. With White's camera looking down, roofs and walls are on screen above the ground they shade, and the characters are upright flat quads, so they'd cast shadows shaped like their sprites, as in the reference.

1. **The camera from the core.** Z-buffered depth is `z/w * 2^23 + 0x7FFE00` (`GPU3D.cpp`, the polygon setup), so it maps straight back to normalized depth, and W-buffered depth is `w` itself (the flags byte says which). Going from there to view space needs the projection and the viewport. The core has `GPU3D::ProjMatrix` and `Viewport`, but those are the geometry engine's current ones, and a game can change them mid-frame (an orthographic menu drawn in 3D last, say). So the core keeps the projection and viewport that most of the frame's polygons used, and hands them over with the depth, through the kept and spare buffers like the flags so a late Blit gets its own frame's. If a game multiplies its camera into the projection, "view space" comes out as world space, which works just as well, since the sun is a direction either way.
2. **The shadow pass.** `melon.screen.sun(strength, { direction, length, steps, softness, color })`: `direction` in view space (up to the script, with a sensible default from the upper right like the reference), `length` how far a shadow can reach, `softness` how far the edge blurs. It runs at DS resolution with a depth check on the way up like C, then darkens towards `color`. Only real 3D takes part, 2D on top stays as it is, and what a step finds off screen or on 2D counts as lit, so shadows thin out towards the screen's edges rather than ending in a hard line.
3. **Checks.** Striaton's buildings cast shadows across the street in one direction, the player and NPCs cast shadows shaped like their sprites, and the shadows don't flicker or swim while walking and scrolling. They fade rather than cut off at the edges, a text box stays unshaded, and the Fold holds 60 FPS at 8x with everything on.

#### Step E: lamps and windows

Glow on chosen things instead of on anything bright. A Lua list of textures (by content hash, since VRAM addresses move) that the OpenGL renderer marks as lights: their polygons write a flag into the attribute buffer, and the depth resolve copies it into the flags byte. Bloom then takes flagged pixels instead of bright ones.

**Spike, 11-22** (desktop, OpenGL, Striaton from the user's save; `--fixed-time` now takes a date and time, e.g. `--fixed-time 2026-11-21T21:00`; `perf_logs/2026-11-22-hd2d-lamps/`):
- Night is the game's lighting, not new textures: every hash is the same at every hour (NPC sprites aside), and light 0 drops from `181614` to `0B0B10`. The start view's mean luma goes 132 at noon, 109 at 17:00, 79 at 21:00.
- The lit windows are a texture of their own, `0x84D7B868` (16x16, format 2, opaque, polygon IDs 0/8/17), lit by light 1 only, which the game switches: dim at noon, orange 17-19, warm 20-23, off 00-16. They write depth. The hash holds walking, at every hour, across state loads, and in Black 2's town with the same light-1 trick.
- Always-bright items (the Pokémon Center front `2F168CB5` and `F25142A9`, Black 2's street lamp `059E4BBF`) look the same day and night, so listing them would glow at noon too.
- Plain bloom can't pick the windows out: they're orange, with luma at most 0.69 at 21:00, so a threshold either misses them or takes the street with them. Their brightest channel does separate them: at most 0.51 at noon and 0.62 at 16:00, 0.78-0.87 in the evening, up to 0.94 at 20-23.
- Every date tried showed "Autumn" on load. That's the save's season, which holds under `--fixed-time` until the first map change; after that the RTC month's season takes over (seen in the game's tree shadows, below), so the other seasons' lighting is still untested.

**Built 11-22** (core `feature/hd2d-camera`, app `feature/hd2d-light`):
- **E1, the flag.** `melon.gpu.light_textures({ hash, ... })` and the desktop's `--light-textures`, kept like the hidden list (at most 64, newest script wins, a script's list beats the desktop's). A listed polygon carries vertex attribute bit 12 (`VtxAttr_Light`, a bit the DS's far-plane clip uses and the renderer dropped), `DepthTag` writes 0.25 for it, and the resolve decodes `>0.75 → 8, >0.375 → 16, >0.125 → 32`. Another camera's tag and a sun shadow win over a light; a texture both hidden and lit stays hidden. `--list-textures` marks `light`, `--depth-shot` counts light pixels.
- **E2, the glow.** Bloom options `lights`, `light_threshold` (0.65) and `light_strength` (1, up to 4), and the desktop's `--bloom-lights [THRESHOLD[,STRENGTH]]`. Each of the bright pass's taps reads its own depth texel; a 3D pixel with flag 32 leaves the luma average and glows by its brightest channel over the same soft knee, times the strength. While `lights` is on, the shading and sun passes leave a flagged pixel alone if its brightest channel is at least the threshold, so lit windows on walls facing away from the sun stay lit and a window that's off is shadowed with its wall. Each is a shader variant, built on first use. `hd2d_white.lua` lists `0x84D7B868` with `light_strength = 2`.

**Checked on the desktop, 11-22** (OpenGL 4x; `perf_logs/2026-11-22-hd2d-lights/`, the before/after pair `bloom/night_off.png` and `night_on.png`):

- At strength 2 the red channel clips on 621 of 15,271 window pixels at 21:00. The 18:00 glow is mild, as the windows peak at 0.78 against 0.65.
- App unit tests: 763, all passing.
